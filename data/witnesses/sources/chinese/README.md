# Chinese witness selection for Rosettapada

Scope: the six-chapter pilot (Pāli chapters 1, 2, 8, 12, 17, and 21), plus the retained Mind page. The dataset covers 99 Pāli verse positions: 1–43, 100–115, 157–166, 221–234, and 290–305. The Chinese column uses historical Chinese recensions, with T210 preferred and explicitly referenced T212/T213 fallbacks. These are not new translations of Pāli. Ken Su's correspondence tables identify the relations; displayed text is checked against the pinned CBETA edition.

`correspondence-mapping.json` retains only correspondence identifiers and ancient verse quotations, omitting modern Pāli translations and commentary. The XML files retain complete, unchanged TEI headers. Every XML body is a derived selection of exactly the retained ancient verse passages: 98 reading records from T210, three from T212, and four from T213. Repeated source stanzas and secondary readings are explicitly identified; these are reading occurrences, not 105 distinct Chinese verses. No unrelated verse or prose commentary is retained. Original chapter labels, decoded historical characters, source wording and punctuation, and Taishō line markers are preserved. `cbeta-provenance.json` records the original-download and selected-snapshot hashes. `verification.json` records each of the 105 checked readings and its exact Taishō span.

`extract.py` resolves CBETA encoded characters through its character declarations. `build_chinese.py` is the compatibility entry point for `expand_selection.py`, which derives the manifest's selection and generates modern Mandarin pinyin while preserving the approved first-43 record values. These preparation scripts are not needed to build or run the static site. Optional regeneration requires Python, lxml, and pypinyin 0.55.0, then `python3 data/witnesses/sources/chinese/build_chinese.py`. The retained snapshots and mapping suffice for this rebuild. To refresh the bounded snapshots from verified complete working copies, supply `--source-directory DIRECTORY`; to append newly authorized chapter correspondence tables, also supply `--correspondence-directory DIRECTORY`. The complete working copies stay outside the repository. No network request is made by these scripts.

Pinyin is an editorial modern Mandarin aid, not historical pronunciation. Contextual corrections cover polyphonic characters and the historical characters 㺃 (gǒu) and 𠎝 (qiān). The new selection also disambiguates 長老 (zhǎng lǎo), 不著名色 (bù zhuó míng sè), 能應 (néng yìng), 伎樂 (jì yuè), and planting/seed readings of 種. Citation tones are used without asserting tone sandhi or word segmentation. Source wording, punctuation, and meaningful recension differences are retained. The table's verse divisions can differ from CBETA punctuation. Exact Taishō line locators accompany every reading.

Coverage: 97 of the 99 Pāli positions have a displayed historical Chinese counterpart. No counterpart to Pāli 42 or 295 is identified in the selected tables across T210/T212/T213. This is not a claim of absence from all Chinese Buddhist literature. The seven fallback references are Pāli 3 → T212 15.7; 4 → T213 14.10; 30 → T213 4.24; 32 → T213 4.32; 34 → T212 32.2; 299 → T213 15.20; and 300 → T212 16.18. The closer body-recollection wording of T213 is deliberately selected for 299 over T212's broader list of recollections. Chinese 29 retains its previous two readings, with the horse counterpart displayed first.

T210 combines recollection of Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha in one historical stanza. At Pāli 296–298, that same whole stanza is repeated as a grouped partial correspondence and explained in Notes. It is not three independent Chinese verses. Alternate T210 counterparts for Pāli 157, 225, 226, 296–298, and 303 are retained with their own references. Six-chapter source coverage does not imply identical contents: the new selection records differences in ritual offering (106–107), own/others' welfare (160, 166), the mother's/ruler's allegory (294), and the snowy-mountain comparison (304), among others. Modern source-table quotations are not substituted for CBETA variants, such as 學推 at 115, 愛會 at 221, and 諸人 at 230.
