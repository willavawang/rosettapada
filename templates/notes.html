Reading notes

What you are reading

The six-chapter selection contains Pairs (1–20), Heedfulness (21–32), Thousands (100–115), Self (157–166), Anger (221–234), and Miscellaneous (290–305). The earlier Mind page (33–43) remains available separately in Contents. Pāli supplies the reading order; Patna, Gāndhārī, Sanskrit, Chinese, and Tibetan parallels appear alongside it. These collections have different arrangements and wording. They are historical witnesses, not six new translations of the same Pāli text.

Bhikkhu Sujato’s English follows the Pāli, not every neighboring tradition. English and the Mahāsaṅgīti Pāli come from SuttaCentral; the translation is dedicated to the public domain under CC0. The earlier two-verse sample is preserved separately with its original working translation and unverified Tibetan placeholder.

Chapter titles and line breaks

Yamakavagga is the chapter of pairs or twin verses; Appamādavagga concerns heedfulness, also rendered diligence or earnestness; Cittavagga concerns the mind, called thought in some older translations. These English titles translate the traditional Pāli chapter names. Our second page uses “Heedfulness”; Sujato’s source heading is “Diligence.”

Each verse begins at the same horizontal level across the sheet. Each tradition retains its own line divisions rather than being forced into the English number of lines. English and Pāli follow the selected SuttaCentral edition; the other readings follow the sources listed below. Line breaks are editorial presentation, not a reconstruction of an original manuscript. Commentary-derived story headings, chapter colophons, and nonprinting formatting markers are omitted.

Alignment is at verse level. Word order, length, imagery, and even the scope of a parallel can differ. The correspondence is not word by word.

Gaps and uncertainties

A dash means no parallel was located in the consulted sources. It does not mean that a tradition has no related teaching. Some Gāndhārī and Patna passages are physically fragmentary; their surviving words and lacunae are retained. Other complete passages correspond only in part. The source table distinguishes those cases.

Dotted underlining marks uncertain or conjecturally restored words in the consulted Indic edition; hovering shows which. Its comparison emphasis is not treated as a conjecture. Legacy private-use transliteration glyphs are displayed as their standard Unicode equivalents, verified against the source font.

Chinese and pinyin

The Chinese column uses the Faju jing (T210) where a counterpart is identified. Where a counterpart is not located in T210, it uses a documented parallel from the Chuyao jing (T212) or Faji yaosong jing (T213); each exact reference is listed below. These recensions are not silently treated as the same collection.

Chinese pinyin is a modern Mandarin reading aid for every displayed Chinese passage, hidden by default. It is not a historical witness or a reconstruction of ancient pronunciation. It can be shown independently of the characters. Rare and context-sensitive readings remain editorial choices.

The comparative sample

Lines in the earlier multilingual sample form a provisional correspondence by sense, not a verified line-by-line concordance. Its spelling, punctuation, source numbering, and transcriptions still require editorial verification. Its English is a working rendering of Pāli and does not translate all six traditions.

The sample labels its witnesses as Pāli Dhp 1–2; Patna PDhp 1–2; Gāndhārī GDhp 201–202; Sanskrit Udānavarga 31.23–24; and Chinese Faju jing, T210 9.1–2. These are inherited references, retained for review.

A difference worth keeping visible

Pāli verse 1 has manomayā (“made by mind”), alongside forms of manojavā in the other Indian witnesses. The Tibetan describes mind as swift. Its final image is a wheel cutting off a head, while the Pāli compares suffering following action to the wheel following an ox’s foot. Those are differences in the consulted texts, not harmonizations supplied for this site. The English continues to translate Pāli.
